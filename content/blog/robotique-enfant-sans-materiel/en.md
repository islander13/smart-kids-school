When people hear "robotics for kids", the reflex is almost always the same: look up which kit to buy. Lego Mindstorms, Arduino, a sensor kit — investments of CHF 150 to 300, before even knowing whether the child will take to it. There's another way in though, less well known but just as serious: virtual robotics.

## What robotics actually teaches

Robotics isn't primarily about motors and cables — it's a way of thinking. A robot needs to perceive its environment (sensors), decide what to do based on what it perceives (conditional logic), and act (motors). This perceive → decide → act loop is the core of the subject, and it can be taught perfectly well on a screen, before ever touching a single cable.

A child programming a virtual robot to follow a line on the ground, stop in front of an obstacle, or react to a detected colour, is working with exactly these concepts: loops ("move forward while the line is detected"), conditions ("if an obstacle is near, then turn"), and a sequence of actions. Physical hardware adds an extra layer of fun, but nothing essential to the understanding that isn't already there.

## How it actually works

In a simulator, the child sees a robot represented on screen, with its sensors (distance, colour, line) shown visually. They write or assemble instructions, run the simulation, see the result immediately, and adjust. The trial-and-error cycle is faster than with a physical robot: no battery to recharge, no part disconnecting, no course to set up. For learning the logic itself, it's often more effective.

## Why start virtual, even if the child eventually wants a real robot

It's not an either-or: virtual robotics is a good starting point precisely because it prepares well for what comes next. A child who has already worked with these concepts virtually approaches a physical kit already understanding what they're doing, rather than following step-by-step instructions without grasping the logic. And if the child ultimately doesn't particularly take to robotics, no equipment will have been bought for nothing.
