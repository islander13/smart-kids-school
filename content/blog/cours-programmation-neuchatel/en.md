Neuchâtel has a particular relationship with precision: it's one of the birthplaces of Swiss watchmaking and microtechnology, a region where meticulous assembly and logical reasoning have been part of the industrial landscape for generations. It's no coincidence that many Neuchâtel parents sense a natural closeness between this heritage and coding, which relies on the same reflexes: breaking a complex problem into precise steps, testing, adjusting, trying again until the mechanism works.

## Naturally fertile ground

A child growing up in a region where precision and microtechnology are part of everyday life — if only through local companies or watchmaking museums — doesn't approach coding logic as a fully abstract concept. Coding means building an invisible mechanism, just as rigorous as a watch movement: every instruction has to click into place at exactly the right moment for the whole thing to work.

## What's actually available for a child in Neuchâtel

You'll find the same three formats as elsewhere in French-speaking Switzerland: after-school group workshops, intensive holiday camps, and regular 100% online classes, one-on-one or in pairs. The Neuchâtel region, smaller than Lausanne or Geneva, generally offers fewer in-person choices — which makes the online format all the more relevant, so you're not limited to what exists within a few kilometres.

## What actually matters when choosing

- **Real progression, not just hours.** A good programme starts with Scratch (from age 7) and moves toward Python in clear stages, rather than treating each session as an isolated module.
- **An individual pace.** Some children need more time on a concept, others move fast and get bored in a group — one-on-one (Solo) or paired (Duo) follow-up solves this directly.
- **Schedules that adapt to the family**, not the other way around: our slots are available between 9am and 7pm.

## Why fully online makes sense in Neuchâtel

For coding, most of the useful interaction — the teacher seeing the child's screen, guiding live, correcting immediately — already happens through a screen, even in an in-person class. Fully online simply removes the travel constraint, which matters particularly for a region where specialised options are more scattered than in a bigger city.
